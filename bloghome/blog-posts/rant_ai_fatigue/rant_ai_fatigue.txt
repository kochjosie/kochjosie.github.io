To make things clear off the bat, I'm not particularly anti-AI or pro-AI. Also, in this post, I will be focusing on LLMs and 
generative AI. I will specify when I'm talking about types of machine learning that are more deterministic in nature.

I'm tired of people using AI to cheat. I'm tired of people using AI as an excuse not to learn. I'm tired of people using 
AI as an excuse to not actually make something creative or stress their brain and learn technical skills. I think its 
disgusting that low-income community leaders are allowing the building of data centers in poorer Americans backyards, despite 
our knowledge that they create noise, light, and chemical pollution and don't belong so close to human habitats. I also think 
AI art and AI music are quite ugly. Not artists and designers who experimented using it as part of their creative process but 
people who use it to generate the art itself. This example is particularly obvious in the gaudy, tacky AI posters that seem 
to litter electric poles and instagram ads nowadays. Its a way to avoid paying a skilled worker for their work and its 
aesthetically ugly.

On the other hand, I'm tired of people who have next to no technical literacy criticizing AI or datacenters. I'm tired of people 
who spend all their time on social media saying we should burn down every data center. I'm tired of people refusing to use AI models 
in ways that make perfect sense, like removing the background from an object in photoshop, largely because they don't know the 
difference between the different kinds of AI. I'm tired of people acting like AI is taking their job.

AI is not taking your job. Employers who don't want to treat employees fairly are taking away entry level jobs, and AI is a convenient 
excuse. If it wasn't AI, it would be something else. You are never safe from someone who doesn't respect you. You are never safe 
from employers who can't think long term, who think that investing in young workers is a waste of time and money. The art students 
I know seem to grasp this concept better. Engineering students on the other hand tend to believe they can out-work or out-study this 
phenomenon. They believe we live in a meritocratic system, which I do not.

As for the environmental argument, this is a huge mixed bag. I think we need to have a real discussion about how mainstream AI use is 
a part of a larger trend of companies producing inefficient bloatware and SaaS startups having no real connection to the fact that 
all of this 'virtual' stuff is based on very real, tangible hardware. Good engineering cares about the sustainability of its make. 
Good engineering does not divorce itself from the physical world. It is deeply embedded in it.

I'm interested in a world where we respect the technical complexity of AI, training small models on well-curated data sets for specific 
purposes. For example, my thermodynamics professor mentioned that he's currently training a model to help him spend less time grading 
our exams. I think this is an appropriate use of the technology. I look forward to a day where he can focus as much as possible on 
his research, his teaching, and personal interests rather than doing administrative tasks that add very little to life. Just like 
before, if there is an error on an exam, a student is able to request a re-grade or bring it up to the professor. This is especially 
true as exams would be graded faster. On top of that, once the model is trained, I would imagine it would function largely quite 
deterministically, meaning it wouldn't be taking up that much computational power.

Anyways, sorry for the rant post. I just felt the need to get these thoughts out somewhere. I may end up re-writting this later in a way 
thats more structured and clear, but, for now, these are my thoughts.
